Entry point: `reconstruct-scene` (`playbook/skills/reconstruct-scene/SKILL.md`)

## Goal

For each target part family, a scene that `scene_build` can produce and that passes the Checks below:

1. **One board frame, one pose.**
   - The board is built in the mocap `NIST_Board1` frame convention.
   - A scene places it with a single board-pose parameter, which this skill only accepts. x, y and yaw come from robot contacts (board-from-contacts, `twin/calib/<recording>.json` `board_contact`); the plate top sits `surface_z` (30.3 mm) above that pose's z (world-frame).
2. **Holes where reality had them.** Hole xy comes from the data, not the NIST CAD. Each hole carries a sigma.
3. **Task frames.** Every hole has `<slug>_hole/entry` (+z into the hole) and `<slug>_hole/bottom`. Every part has `<slug>/tip` and `<slug>/grasp` (+z along insertion, +y the finger-closing axis). Conventions: `playbook/skills/mujoco`.
4. **Success predicates from sim state only.**
   - They live in `twin/predicates.py`; thresholds are MJCF numerics.
   - `depth = (tip − entry)·z_entry`, `lateral = |(tip − entry) − depth·z_entry|`, `tilt = ∠(z_tip, z_entry)`.
   - `seated = depth ≥ seat_depth − 1 mm ∧ lateral ≤ clearance + 0.2 mm ∧ tilt ≤ 3°`.
   - `engaged = depth > 2 mm ∧ lateral ≤ tol`.
   - `axial_resist` = the board's contact force on the part along −z_entry.
5. **The holes behave.** Aligned parts go in. Parts offset beyond the clearance jam with a stiff stage, or beyond clearance + chamfer with a compliant one. The answer is the same at dt/2.
6. **A spread of worlds.** `sample_seed` draws one physically consistent world from the per-parameter uncertainty, and `params.json` records every value used.

## Inputs

- `twin/assets/board_layout.json`, generated by `board_layout` / `python -m twin.layout`: per-part hole xy in the board frame from **train-split** REASSEMBLE inserts, plus a grid cross-check and the plate outline. `scene_build` still reads hole xy from this file. Replay, the practice starts and `measure_b0` use the contact layout instead (board-from-contacts: `runs/claude/run-standin-1/board/layout_contact.json` and the per-recording files). The two differ by 1–12 mm per hole (9 of 17 holes by more than 5 mm); the contact layout matches the NIST plate at 0.76 mm RMS.
- `data/reassemble/` signals and `data/reassemble-tudata/poses/` (mocap board pose per recording). Their caveats are in `docs/research/reassemble-data-notes.md`.
- Catalog `twin/parts.py`. Sources are cited in every parameter's `source`; `scene_parts` prints them.
  - Pegs: paper App. B (round 4/8/12/16 mm; keys 4, 8×7, 12×8, 16×10). Peg N = size 4N, confirmed by grip width at release: 1.6 / 6.1 / 11.0 / 14.8 mm for round pegs 1–4.
  - Round holes: the NIST drill bits 4.1 / 8.1 / 12.2 / 16.5 mm.
  - Key holes: the NIST laser plate (`runs/scene/experiments/stl_holes.log`).
  - Chamfer: 0.1 × the largest side on the leading end (NIST STL; REASSEMBLE unverified).
- `playbook/skills/mujoco` for how to model, step and check.

## Approach

Guidance, not a recipe. Try things, measure them, and write the numbers into Lessons.

1. **Put precision where outcomes depend on it.** Ask which parameter changes whether an attempt succeeds, and model only that exactly.

   | Exact (primitives, sensitive, sampled) | Approximate (sampled, flagged) | Cosmetic |
   |---|---|---|
   | hole size and shape, peg section, chamfer, hole axis, friction, plate thickness (sets tilt tolerance), seat depth (sets success) | hole xy (matters for replays, not for practice in sim), board surface height, grasp height on the part, density | plate outline, base and standoffs, colours |

   Exact here means MuJoCo primitives: N-gon wall boxes for round holes, 4 walls for keys (`twin/board.py`). Meshes and SDFs are for shapes that matter less; the mujoco skill has the comparison.

2. **Hole positions from the data.** (This release-median method made `board_layout.json`. board-from-contacts' line model replaced it for the twin's holes.)
   - Use the TCP (`ee_pose`, the 0.174 m tool point) at **gripper release** in each successful Insert, mapped into that recording's mocap board frame, then take the median per part.
   - Use the train split only, unless the orchestrator froze other splits.
   - Cross-check against the NIST 75 mm grid and say what disagrees.
   - Do **not** project along the tool axis: it made estimates worse (Lessons).
   - Keys take their yaw from the gripper yaw (fingers close across the long side), snapped to 90°.
3. **Uncertainty is part of the model.**
   - Every number is a `P(value, sigma, unit, source, sensitive)`.
   - A sample must stay physical: hole ≥ peg + 10 µm; seat depth ≥ plate thickness; seat depth ≤ the plate top's height above the table at the given board pose (`scene_build` clamps sampled worlds and warns on nominal ones).
   - The spread is for practice and for replay-validate to narrow. Do not tune it to make a policy pass.
4. **Verification ladder**, cheapest first:
   1. `scene_build` (nominal, `part_init: seated`) → `scene_check` ok.
   2. `insertion_probe` stiff and compliant, `offsets_mm: auto` → `geometry_ok` and `half_dt_agrees`.
   3. The same on a few `sample_seed`s.
   4. `part_init: above` (parts drop in under gravity) → `scene_check` ok, warnings only for impact transients.
   5. `twin.warp_check` at the batch size practice will use.
   6. `scene_render` and an overlay with the world-frame agent's cameras. **Board height and plate thickness can only be pinned from images.**
5. **Probe semantics.**
   - The probe rig is a fixture in the model from t = 0: a rigid grasp at `<slug>/grasp`, Cartesian slides, a force-limited z servo. The part moves only through contact, so it does not break the rules on welds or external forces.
   - Stiff lateral → the seat/jam threshold is the clearance.
   - 500 N/m (REASSEMBLE's impedance default) → the threshold is clearance + chamfer.
   - `tilt_deg` holds a crooked grasp. A tilted peg passes only below about 2c/t_plate (0.64° for 0.05 mm clearance in a 9 mm plate).
6. **Extending to a new family** (`FUTURE_FAMILIES` in `twin/parts.py`):
   1. Add catalog entries with sources and sigmas.
   2. Add a hole builder in `board.py` (keep the entry, bottom, tip and grasp conventions).
   3. Write a probe expectation (what aligned and misaligned should do).
   4. Then run the same ladder. The family-specific needs are below.
   - **Gears** (small/medium/large; module 1, 20/40/60 teeth, 10 mm bore) slide over fixed 10 mm shafts.
     - Model the shaft as a cylinder and the bore as an N-gon ring, exactly as a round hole turned inside-out: a part with a hole over a fixed peg.
     - Teeth matter only for meshing with the neighbouring gear. Data: small gear failed 4/4 in episode 0. Model teeth as a ring of boxes, or the CPU-only `mujoco.sdf.gear` plugin (no Warp).
     - The layout has gear xy (shafts are not on the 75 mm grid).
   - **Bolt 4 (M16 nut).**
     - Threads need `mujoco.sdf.nut`/`bolt` (CPU only).
     - Or approximate: a hex prism nut with a cylindrical bore over a cylinder, plus a twist predicate (yaw travelled × pitch 2 mm = axial advance). Choose by whether practice needs real thread engagement.
   - **Connectors** (BNC, D-SUB, Ethernet, USB, waterproof).
     - Fits are keyed shells. Build the mating surfaces from boxes (housing walls, keys); build cosmetic bodies from coacd pieces of the NIST STLs (mm, board coordinates).
     - BNC and waterproof need a twist-lock predicate. The Ethernet latch is a compliant feature: start rigid with a detent force threshold.
     - REASSEMBLE's redesigned Ethernet/USB holders are not public; take their geometry from images.

## Checks

A watcher runs these on the actor's outputs. They match `playbook/validation.yaml` B1.

|---|---|---|

| B1.aligned_insert_rate | `insertion_probe {part, lateral: stiff}` and `compliant`, `offsets_mm: auto`, every part in scope | every offset below the threshold → `seated` |
| B1.misaligned_jam_rate | same runs | every offset beyond the threshold → not seated |
| B1.dt_halving_agreement | same runs (`halved_timestep: true`) | `half_dt_agrees: true` for all |

## Lessons

**2026-10-24, claude-opus-5.5 stand-in, round and square pegs 1–4 (caller `build-twin-dev` in `runs/calls/20261024.jsonl`).**

**Results**

- **Probe campaign, nominal world** (`runs/scene/probe_campaign_20261024_final.txt`, JSON and plots in `runs/scene/probe/*_20261024-1735*`):
  - 16/16 probe sets `geometry_ok` (8 parts × stiff and compliant, 4 offsets each), and 16/16 `half_dt_agrees` at dt = 1 ms vs 0.5 ms.
  - Clearances per side: round pegs 0.05 / 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.25 mm; keys 0.055, 0.074×0.079, 0.069×0.089, 0.106×0.126 mm.
  - Stiff stage: seated at 0.8c, jammed at c + max(0.25c, 0.04 mm).
  - Compliant stage (500 N/m): seated up to 0.8(c + chamfer), for example 1.48 mm for round peg 4, and jammed beyond.
  - Jam depths match the geometric value (chamfer − overlap) within 0.02 mm, for example round peg 4 at 1.56 vs 1.54 mm.
  - Resting penetration is 0.026–0.038 mm at 10 N. Wall time is 0.02–0.1 s per 1.4 s of simulated probe.
- **Sampled worlds.**
  - Probes (seeds 0–4, round peg 1 and square peg 2, stiff and compliant): 20/20 `geometry_ok`, 20/20 dt/2 agree, with thresholds following each sample (round peg 1 clearance 0.022–0.104 mm). `runs/scene/probe_sampled_20261024_final.txt`.
  - Scene checks: nominal and seeds 0–9 → all `ok`, all 8 pegs seated, final penetration ≤ 0.0004 mm, drift ≤ 0.0014 mm (`runs/scene/pegs_seed*/`, `runs/scene/pegs_nominal/`).
- **Gravity drop.** All 8 pegs released 10 mm above their holes fall in and seat (`runs/scene/pegs_above_1ms`). On Warp, 1024/1024 worlds did the same, matching CPU depth to 0.0001 mm at 337 k steps/s.

**Problems found and fixed**

- **dt = 2 ms is not converged for these fits.**
  - A 4×4 key overlapping the rim by 0.024 mm went 1.4 mm deep at 2 ms, against 0.398 mm at 1 and 0.5 ms (geometric 0.376 mm).
  - MuJoCo's default contacts slid it 5.7 mm at every dt.
  - Fixed: contact parameters in `twin/board.py`, dt defaults to 1 ms (`runs/scene/experiments/contact_params.log`).
  - Overlaps below about 0.03 mm (the resting penetration) still give dt-dependent depths. Seed 3's 0.0188 mm overlap gave 1.63 vs 0.71 mm, so the `auto` jam offset now overlaps by ≥ 0.04 mm.
- **The vertical stack was wrong at first.**
  - With seat depth 20 mm (from the TCP drop after first contact), seated pegs ended 5.4 mm inside the table at the mocap board height; `scene_check` flagged it.
  - The mocap board origin sits at world z 3.6 / 8.2 / 16.9 mm (p5/p50/p95, 153 recordings), which fits a 9 mm plate lying on the table with the origin at its top.
  - Now seat depth = plate thickness = 9 ± 3 mm and surface_z = 0 ± 5 mm. (Superseded by the 2026-10-24 b2-replay entry below: surface_z 30.3 mm, seat depth 20 mm.)
  - Still unexplained: the TCP drops 16–21 mm after first contact (max per peg, `insert_travel.log`), and at insert the TCP sits 16 mm higher than at pick (median, MAD 7 mm, `pick_vs_insert_height.log`). Both point to the peg slipping or pivoting in the fingers. Pin the stack from side-view images.

**Hole positions from data** (`twin/assets/board_layout.json`; 80 train events, episodes 1–7 and 13)

- Per-part sigma is 1–4 mm, except round peg 4 (n = 2, sigma_y 11 mm).
- A free-pitch 4×4 grid fits the 8 pegs with 2.9 mm RMS. The pitch comes out at 77.3 × 74.8 mm against NIST's 75, so the REASSEMBLE board reuses NIST's grid with a different part assignment. The fit puts the plate corner at (−10, 0) mm in the mocap frame.
- Keys sit with their long side along board y (measured yaw 86–92°).
- **Per-event scatter is large.** A single release's TCP xy sits a median **9.3 mm** from the hole estimated from the other recordings (p90 21 mm, `layout_loo.log`).
  - A per-recording rigid board correction only gets that to 6.7 mm (`layout_per_recording.log`).
  - Projecting along the tool axis makes it 13–15 mm (`layout_tilt_projection.log`).
  - So the scatter is mostly per event: where the part sits along the finger pads, and the tool tilting about a peg that stays upright.
  - **Consequence for validation.yaml B0.board_err_mm (≤ 3 mm, per event):** it cannot be met from TCP data. Propose measuring per-part medians, or entry vs tip in replays. (Adopted in v2: median of per-part errors.)
- **The real tool tilts 3–5° at release, but in the twin a 4 mm peg jams at 1° tilt and enters at 0.3°.** Geometry forbids more than about 0.64° in a 9 mm plate (`probe_tilt.log`). The real peg must pivot in the fingers, so the gripper model needs rotational compliance in the grasp. This is for author-tools and replay-validate.

**Gotchas**

- Unchamfered pegs (`overrides: {chamfer_frac: 0}`) jam at 0.27 mm instead of 0. That is a contact-normal artefact; the outcome is right.
- Sampled worlds can put the seat below the table; `scene_build` clamps sampled worlds and warns on nominal ones.

**Open**

- Board height: done (plate top 30.3 ± 4 mm above the mocap z, world-frame). Plate thickness and chamfers on the real pegs still need images.
- Grasp compliance needs gripper work.
- B0.board_err_mm metric: changed in v2 (per-part median).
- Gears, bolt 4 and connectors are not built yet (see Approach §6).
- **2026-10-24** (claude:b2-replay): Vertical stack corrected (twin/board.py, twin/parts.py). Plate top +30.3 mm (+-4) above the mocap board z (camera triangulation; the plate sits on an aluminium frame). Seat depth 20 +- 3 mm (episode-0 Remove pull-outs 17-23 mm, a TCP rise that does not depend on the TCP offset). Pads 9 +- 5 mm below a peg's top end (onset timing with the corrected hand). This resolves the 'TCP drops 16-21 mm after contact' conflict: the hand was 22.9 mm too far from the flange in base.xml.
