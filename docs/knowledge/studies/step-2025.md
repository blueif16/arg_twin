# STEP: policy comparison with near-optimal stopping (Snyder et al., RSS 2025)

`[step-2025]` · status: verified · local: `data/refs/step-2025.pdf`

Read 2026-10-24 from arXiv 2503.10966 (Snyder, Hancock, Badithela, Dixon, Miller, Ambruş, et al.), published at RSS 2025 (doi 10.15607/RSS.2025.XXI.077). Every number is theirs. Lines starting "For us" are our reading.

## Setup

- **Question:** is policy π1 better than π0 on a binary success metric?
- **Data:** trials arrive one pair at a time. Each trial must be an i.i.d. draw of start state and observation.
- **Method:** STEP precomputes decision regions over (successes of π0, successes of π1, n). After each pair it says continue, reject the null, or accept / fail to decide. Type-I error is controlled for any stopping time up to N_max.
- **Baselines:** the sequential tests SAVI and Lai, an oracle SPRT that knows the true rates, and Barnard's batch test run repeatedly.

## Results

- Running Barnard's test after every pair breaks Type-I error control. That is p-hacking by peeking.
- STEP needs up to 32% fewer trials than the sequential baselines while keeping error control; in multi-task sim comparisons it saves more than 160 rollouts.
- **Stopping times (α = 0.05 on hardware, 0.01 in sim):**

  | Task | p0 → p1 | N_max | SAVI | Lai | STEP | Oracle |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | FoldRedTowel (real) | 0.56 → 0.92 | 50 | 20 | 17 | 19 | 17 |
  | CleanUpSpill (real) | 0.28 → 0.80 | 50 | 7 | 8 | 8 | 7 |
  | CarrotOnPlate (real) | 0.59 → 0.68 | 100 | fail to decide | – | – | – |
  | EggplantInBasket (sim) | 0.40 → 0.56 | 500 | 192 | 125 | 131 | 128 |
  | StackCube (sim) | 0.00 → 0.03 | 500 | 329 | 417 | 225 | 135 |

- **Large gaps settle in about 10–20 pairs.** A gap of about 9 points was undecidable at 100 trials per policy.

## Limits

- Binary outcomes only.
- The i.i.d. assumption is essential: the authors followed the best practices of [empirical-science-2024] to make it hold on hardware.
- "Fail to decide" means the gap is too small for the budget, not that the policies are equal.

## For us

- For us: when comparing attempt 1 with attempt N (H1/H2 in validation.yaml), fix N_max and α before running. Use a sequential test if we may stop early; otherwise run a single batch test (exact McNemar on paired starts) once.
- For us: frozen held-out starts drawn from a fixed distribution satisfy the i.i.d. assumption in the twin far better than hardware does. Twin comparisons can therefore be rigorous cheaply. What they cannot show is real transfer.
- For us: gaps under about 10 points need hundreds of trials. In the twin that is affordable; for brain-token cost per trial it may not be, so state the budget and the smallest gap it can detect up front.
